# Research Brief: Competition & Rubric Intelligence for VoiceLink at Intel AI Global Impact Festival 2025

## Question
What evidence, citations, competitive intelligence, and framework references will maximize VoiceLink's score against the exact three-metric, 93-point rubric (Enriching Lives 30 / AI Innovation 30 / Intel Tech 30 / IDR 3)?

## Rubric structure confirmed from `/Users/tanayvinaykya/VoiceLink/docs/rubric-2025.pdf`
- **Metric 01 (30 pts):** Problem significance (clarity 0-3, evidence-exists 0-3, time-critical 0-3), Diversity & Inclusion (audience 0-3, accessibility/usability 0-3, scalability/GTM 0-3), Impact (depth 0-3, scale 0-3, duration 0-3, SDG alignment 0-3).
- **Metric 02 (30 pts):** AI necessity (0-2), effective/pervasive use (0-3), originality classification (0-3), model appropriateness (0-3), sub-domain understanding — ML/DL/CV/NLP/GenAI (0-2), data provenance clarity (0-2), **ethics 0-6 (single biggest line item)**, Readiness: prototype (0-3) / controlled testing (0-3) / full deployment (0-3).
- **Metric 03 (30 pts):** Intel resource use breadth (0-3) + justification (0-4); Intel AI-optimized hardware appropriateness (0-3) + type (0-3) + lifecycle extent (0-4); Intel AI software appropriateness (0-3) + type (0-3) + lifecycle extent (0-4).
- **IDR (3 pts):** binary — is the project affiliated with Intel Digital Readiness / any Intel program.

## Metric 01 — Enriching Lives: Problem Evidence (confidence: HIGH)

**Prevalence / who is affected:**
- CDC (ADDM Network, released April 2025): **1 in 31** 8-year-old children in the U.S. identified with autism spectrum disorder (2022 surveillance year), up from 1 in 36 in 2020. [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/ss/ss7402a1.htm), [SARRC summary](https://autismcenter.org/autismprevalence/)
- CDC's first-ever "profound autism" statistic: **26.7% of children with ASD have profound autism** (nonverbal/minimally verbal or IQ<50); population rate 4.6 per 1,000 8-year-olds (2016 data). [Autism Science Foundation](https://autismsciencefoundation.org/press_releases/cdc-profound-autism-statistics/), [MIDB](https://midb.umn.edu/news/study-charts-changing-prevalence-profound-and-non-profound-autism)
- Independent estimates converge: **~25-30% of autistic children are nonverbal or minimally verbal** (fewer than 30 functional words). [Golden Care Therapy](https://goldencaretherapy.com/what-percentage-of-autism-is-nonverbal/)
- Beyond autism: ASHA/complex-communication-needs (CCN) literature estimates **1.3-1.5% of the U.S. population (4-5 million people)** cannot reliably meet daily communication needs with natural speech; **2.1% of children with disabilities** require AAC. Largest diagnostic groups needing AAC: dementia (23.2%), Parkinson's (22.7%), ASD (18.9%), learning disabilities (13.3%), stroke/aphasia (9.9%). UK estimate: 0.5% of population. [CommunicationFIRST "AAC Counts II"](https://communicationfirst.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/AAC-Counts-Report-Part-2-What-Existing-Data-Can-and-Cannot-Tell-Us.pdf), [ASHA Practice Portal](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/augmentative-and-alternative-communication/)

**Cost/access barrier evidence:**
- Dedicated speech-generating devices (SGDs) cost **$4,000-$12,000**; non-dedicated iPad-based AAC apps run **$800-$2,500**. Reimbursement approval cycles take **3-18 months**. [speechpathology.org](https://www.speechpathology.org/articles/articles-aac-devices/), [Tandfonline device-abandonment article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2023.2199859)
- **Abandonment: 30-35% within the first year**; broader assistive-tech literature cites up to a third abandoned shortly after acquisition and up to 75% "never used successfully" — attributed to poor training/follow-up and boards that don't match real context. [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07434618.2023.2199859), [PMC multi-stakeholder barriers study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11197385/)

**Time-criticality (rubric item 3):**
- AAC users communicate at **~10-18 words/minute vs. 125-185 wpm for natural speech** — roughly 10% of natural conversational speed, causing conversational partners to disengage or perceive users as less capable when device response lags. [ASHA Leader](https://leader.pubs.asha.org/do/10.1044/2020-1016-aac-awareness/full/), [word-prediction rate study, UDel](https://www.eecis.udel.edu/~mccoy/publications/2008/trnka08at.pdf)
- Developmental critical-window argument: children who remain minimally verbal past age 5 are at high risk of **persistent, severe communication deficits** affecting social/educational outcomes for life — creating urgency to intervene during the preschool/school-age window. Meta-analytic evidence (2024, J. Autism Dev. Disord.) shows aided-AAC combined with naturalistic behavioral intervention produces the strongest gains in spoken language for minimally verbal children — i.e., the *context* in which AAC vocabulary appears matters, not just the device. [Springer meta-analysis 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-024-06382-7), [PMC SMART trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4030683/)

This bundle directly supports VoiceLink's premise (pre-populated, context-driven vocabulary reduces the time-cost of AAC use and targets the exact deficit — communication rate/latency — documented in the literature).

## Metric 01 — SDG Alignment (confidence: HIGH)
Cite by number and target text, not just the goal:
- **SDG 3 — Good Health & Well-Being**: broadly cited for disability/health equity; target 3.4/3.8 language used for NCDs/universal health coverage — weaker direct fit, use as secondary framing only. [WHO SDG data](https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/themes/sustainable-development-goals)
- **SDG 4 — Quality Education**, Target 4.5/4.a: eliminate disparities and build inclusive, disability-sensitive learning facilities/environments for vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities — **strong, direct fit** (VoiceLink surfaces vocabulary in real classroom/social contexts). [UN DESA — SDGs and Disability](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/sustainable-development-goals-sdgs-and-disability)
- **SDG 10 — Reduced Inequalities**, Target **10.2**: "empower and promote the social, economic and political inclusion of all... irrespective of age, sex, disability..." by 2030 — **strongest, most literal fit** for an AAC communication-equity project. [UN Enable](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/about-us/sustainable.development-goals-sdgs-and-disability.html)
- Recommend leading with SDG 10 (target 10.2, verbatim quote) + SDG 4 (target 4.5) as co-primary, SDG 3 as tertiary.

## Metric 02 — AI Innovation: Competitive Benchmark (confidence: HIGH for Sightline, MEDIUM for others)

**Sightline (2025 Global Award winner, USA — Chilukuri/Bedson/Patel):** Blind-navigation smart glasses on ESP32-S3, matching Apple Depth Pro metric depth maps to YOLOv8 object detections to build a spatially-aware system prompt for an LLM; voice-interaction app. Intel-specific claims: **Intel Core i7 edge inference** ("faster than competitive GPUs, reduces system load"), **OpenVINO** as the "critical" edge-acceleration toolkit, **Intel Neural Compressor** applying post-training INT8 quantization for a claimed **2-4x inference speedup** and reduced memory footprint, with **NNCF** tested as an alternate quantization path, and Intel DevCloud slated for future citizen testing. Privacy design: only textual (not raw image) data leaves the edge device to any cloud LLM. Readiness status: explicitly "experimental/development-focused," validated on COCO, no full deployment claimed. [GitHub README](https://github.com/JoshuaChil/SightLine), [Intel Newsroom](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-recognizes-young-ai-changemakers-at-global-impact-festival)

**2025 AI-for-Accessibility special award winners** (a separate, smaller-scope award track): **Ishaara** (India) — Indian Sign Language ↔ text/speech across 12 regional languages; **DysTherapy** (Turkey) — dyslexia education app; **Your Voice** (Vietnam) — Vietnamese Sign Language → text/speech via CV + animated avatar, targeting Vietnam's 2.5M+ deaf/mute population. None of these use a wearable+context-fusion architecture like VoiceLink — they are single-modality translation tools, suggesting VoiceLink's multi-sensor fusion (scene + transcript + personal context) is differentiated. [Intel press release via businesswire/intc](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1646/intel-celebrates-ai-accessibility-and-enables-the-next)

**2024/2023 winners for pattern-matching judging expectations:** urban-soil digital twin, YOLOv8-based multi-disease medical diagnostics (20k images), locally-deployed-LLM personalized tutoring platform, item-tracking smart cabinet (2024); "Here-Charlie" robotic guide dog (IR/ultrasonic/LIDAR sensor fusion) and CV-based eye-tracking communication system for ALS patients (2023) — this last one is the closest historical analog to VoiceLink (assistive communication for a nonverbal/low-motor population). [Intel Newsroom](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-celebrates-ai-innovations-by-students-with-global-impact-festival)

**Judging process (2025):** Intel evaluates on "AI innovation, responsible technology use and social impact," and **every winner undergoes an Intel-conducted AI ethics audit** before being finalized — reinforcing that the 0-6 ethics line item is not a formality but an actual audited gate. 32 countries, six Global winners split across two age brackets (13-17, 18+) in the 2025 cycle (other sourcing says 109 projects/33 countries/47 awards total — minor source discrepancy, flag as LOW confidence on exact counts). [Intel Newsroom](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-recognizes-young-ai-changemakers-at-global-impact-festival)

## Metric 02 — Ethics (0-6 pts, biggest single line item) (confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH)
Named frameworks worth citing directly in the submission:
- **EU AI Act** risk tiers (prohibited / high-risk / limited-risk / minimal-risk), in force July 12 2024, high-risk obligations phasing in late 2026-2027. Annex III does not explicitly list AAC/assistive tech as high-risk, but biometric categorization and profiling of vulnerable users (nonverbal disabled individuals) is adjacent territory — worth a sentence positioning VoiceLink as "designed to the high-risk bar" even though not strictly mandated. [artificialintelligenceact.eu Art. 6](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/6/), [Glocert playbook](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/guides/eu-ai-act-risk-classification-playbook/)
- **Bystander privacy in wearable cameras** is an actively researched problem: bystanders "lack an explicit and reliable mechanism to manage how their image/video is captured" — this is directly the VoiceLink scenario (device captures people around the AAC user). Cite this literature to justify the CLAUDE.md guardrails (local-only inference, ephemeral buffers, no persistent recording, hardware kill switch, team-only testing until proxy consent exists) as a considered response to a documented open problem, not an afterthought. [arXiv "Mind the Gap"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04930), [Frontiers Digital Health 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1431246/full)
- **WCAG 2.2 (W3C)** POUR principles (Perceivable/Operable/Understandable/Robust); WCAG 2.2 explicitly added guidance for cognitive disabilities but the standard itself notes even AAA conformance does not guarantee accessibility for cognitive/language/learning disability users — cite this caveat to show nuanced understanding rather than a checkbox claim. [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [W3C Cognitive Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/cognitive/)
- No single "IEEE assistive-AI ethics standard" surfaced as a clean citation (IEEE 7000-series is general AI ethics, not assistive-specific) — flag as **unknown/LOW**, do not over-claim an IEEE-specific assistive framework exists.

## Metric 02 — Readiness of Solution evidence shapes (confidence: MEDIUM)
- **Prototype tier** artifacts that scored well historically: working end-to-end demo video/repo (Sightline: GitHub repo + glasses hardware demo).
- **Controlled/test-environment tier**: explicit statement of test population and conditions (Sightline states COCO-dataset validation + planned DevCloud citizen testing — i.e., they were transparent that they had NOT yet reached this tier, and were not penalized off the podium for it, suggesting honesty about tier boundaries is safe and possibly rewarded over-claiming).
- **Full deployment tier**: none of the surfaced winners (Sightline, Ishaara, Your Voice, Here-Charlie) claimed full-scale live deployment for their target audience — this tier appears to be the hardest for any student team to score, and VoiceLink should not expect to claim it; a credible "team-only test environment, path-to-consented-deployment plan" answer is competitive with what won before.

## Metric 03 — Intel Technology Usage (confidence: MEDIUM — inferred from rubric text + Sightline pattern, not an Intel style guide)
Rubric explicitly rewards: (a) breadth across the **full lifecycle** (dev → test → deploy) not a single stage, (b) an explicit **written justification** for each Intel tool choice (why OpenVINO here, why not just PyTorch), (c) **AI-specific hardware** (Gaudi/Goya/Movidius) scores higher on "type of hardware" than general-purpose Core/Xe — VoiceLink's stated Mac-now/Intel-later roadmap should be reframed for the submission to show Intel usage at build time, not just "later": e.g., use **Intel DevCloud** for benchmarking during development now, and explicitly plan **OpenVINO + NNCF quantization** (the same 2-4x pattern Sightline cited) for the Whisper/Qwen2.5-VL conversion — this is a direct, low-effort parallel to a project that already won.

## Metric — IDR Participation (3 pts) (confidence: LOW-MEDIUM, enrollment mechanics not confirmed)
- Two Intel programs exist: **AI for Youth** (ages 13-20, grades 3-12, 250 hours of curriculum covering ML/CV/NLP + AI ethics/bias/privacy, culminating in social-impact projects) and **AI for Future Workforce**. [Intel AI for Youth](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/corporate/artificial-intelligence/digital-readiness-ai-for-youth.html)
- Both direct fetches of Intel's enrollment pages returned HTTP 403 (site blocks automated fetch) — **could not confirm exact self-enrollment steps, timeline, or whether an individual/unaffiliated team can join without a school/government partner.** Program appears delivered largely through **institutional partnerships** (schools, ministries — e.g. India: 6,000+ schools, 1.6M students) rather than open individual signup. [IndiaAI coverage](https://indiaai.gov.in/news/making-ai-accessible-for-all-with-intel-digital-readiness-programs)
- **Action needed, not confirmed by research**: verify directly with Intel Festival organizers/FAQ whether completing any public Intel AI course (e.g., an Intel Skills for Innovation / Coursera-hosted Intel course) counts as "IDR participation" for the 3-pt line, since this is the fastest path for an independent team without a school partnership. Flag as unknown.

## Recommendations
1. Lead problem statement with the 1-in-31 / 26.7% profound-autism / 4-5M CCN-population trio, cited to CDC + CommunicationFIRST, plus the 10-18 wpm vs 125-185 wpm rate gap as the time-criticality hook.
2. Cite SDG 10 target 10.2 verbatim as primary, SDG 4 target 4.5 as secondary.
3. In the ethics section, explicitly cite bystander-privacy literature and EU AI Act risk tiers to justify each CLAUDE.md guardrail (kill switch, ephemeral buffers, team-only testing) as an engineered response to a named, documented risk — not a generic privacy statement.
4. Mirror Sightline's Intel-usage narrative structure (hardware choice → OpenVINO → quantization/Neural-Compressor or NNCF → measured speedup number) for the Whisper/Qwen2.5-VL port; start using **Intel DevCloud** now during development, not just "later," to score the lifecycle-breadth items in Metric 03.
5. Resolve the IDR 3-pt question directly with festival organizers/FAQ rather than guessing — do not assume self-enrollment is possible without confirmation.
6. In Readiness-of-Solution language, be transparent that VoiceLink is at prototype/team-only-controlled-test tier — this is consistent with (not penalized relative to) prior winners, so do not over-claim deployment.

## Risks
- Source discrepancy on 2025 festival scale (109 projects/33 countries/47 awards vs. 32 countries/six Global winners) — reconcile with the official winners page before quoting a specific number publicly (direct fetch of intel.com winner pages returned 403; only WebSearch snippets available).
- IEEE-specific "assistive AI ethics standard" does not clearly exist — do not cite one that can't be verified.
