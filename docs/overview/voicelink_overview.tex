\begin{abstract}
\noindent People who speak through AAC boards are limited to whatever someone
programmed in advance. VoiceLink is a body-worn device that senses the room and the
conversation around its user and rewrites the board in about ten milliseconds, so the
words that fit this moment are already there. This document describes the system, how
it is evaluated, what it demonstrably does, and --- deliberately --- what it does not.
Every figure quoted here was checked against a committed artifact rather than against a
summary document.
\end{abstract}

%==============================================================================
\section{What the system does}
%==============================================================================

An AAC board is a grid of tappable words. A conventional board is fixed: someone
programs it ahead of time, and if the right word is not on it, the user cannot say the
thing they mean. Programming a board for every situation a person will encounter is
impossible in principle.

VoiceLink surfaces the board's contents from context. The board holds ten slots. Four
are pinned permanently --- \emph{yes}, \emph{no}, \emph{I need help}, \emph{wait} ---
chosen as four distinct communicative functions so that a bad model output can never
strand someone without them. The other six are selected per moment.

The concrete demonstration is a situation no pre-programmed board would have anticipated:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Scene:} a classroom, a teacher at a whiteboard with a diagram of a plant.\\
\textbf{Heard:} ``What do plants take in from the air?''\\
\textbf{Board gains:} \emph{carbon dioxide}, \emph{oxygen and water}, \emph{sun},
\emph{I am not sure}, \emph{can you repeat that}, \emph{I know this one}.
\end{quote}

Or, at a family meal, ``Who do you want to sit next to?'' surfaces \emph{aunt} ---
a word that is not in the system's fixed vocabulary at all, and reaches the board by a
different mechanism described in Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}.

%==============================================================================
\section{Architecture: three devices, one seam}
%==============================================================================

\begin{itemize}
  \item A \textbf{wearable capture device} (ESP32-S3, worn as a clip or pendant) senses
  and streams. It performs no processing whatsoever.
  \item A \textbf{Mac} runs all of the intelligence.
  \item A \textbf{tablet or phone} displays the board through a web page.
\end{itemize}

The wearable talks only to the Mac. The tablet talks only to the Mac. All processing is
local; nothing leaves the Mac in normal operation.

\subsection{The seam}

Every model call in the entire system passes through one function with a fixed
signature:

\begin{center}
\code{rank\_vocabulary(scene, transcript, context) -> ranked\_words}
\end{center}

The wearable never calls a model. The web application never calls a model. They reach
this function only through the Mac server. No model is ever wired directly into a route,
the firmware, or the UI.

This single constraint is what allowed the inference backend to be replaced --- PyTorch
to OpenVINO, Apple silicon to Intel --- by changing a configuration flag rather than
rewriting the system. It was not a refactor applied later; it was the first
architectural decision, and it is the reason the Intel port exists at all.

%==============================================================================
\section{How a board is produced}
%==============================================================================

The wearable streams VGA JPEG frames at a 2000\,ms cadence and 16\,kHz PCM audio in
512-sample chunks. Nothing is written to disk.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Listen.} Whisper \code{base.en} transcribes locally. A change gate skips
  the vision model when the scene has not moved, so the camera path costs nothing while
  the user sits still.
  \item \textbf{Look.} A vision--language model writes a one-sentence scene description.
  \item \textbf{Over-generate.} Rather than predicting the single likeliest reply, the
  ranker produces candidates spanning nine communicative functions (request, reject,
  comment, question, share-information, express-feeling, social, direct-attention,
  clarify) plus concrete topics visible in the scene. Breadth is the objective: one of
  the offered directions should be the one the user actually means.
  \item \textbf{Select for spread.} Maximal marginal relevance (MMR) selects six
  candidates that are relevant \emph{and} mutually different, so the board does not fill
  with six paraphrases of one answer. When a question has just been asked of the user,
  answer candidates receive a reserved quota.
  \item \textbf{Pin and serve.} The four core words are pinned, pictogram symbols are
  resolved from a local set, and the board is pushed to the tablet over a WebSocket.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Interaction logging.} Every vocabulary tap is recorded together with a
snapshot of the scene, transcript and context that produced the board. This data cannot
be reconstructed retroactively, so it has been logged since the first working UI. The
store currently holds 121 rows, all from project team members.

\section{The models}
%==============================================================================

All models run locally.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Role} & \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Notes} \\
\midrule
Speech        & \code{whisper-base.en}      & faster-whisper, or INT8 via OpenVINO \\
Scene         & \code{Qwen2.5-VL-3B}        & 4-bit; one sentence per changed scene \\
Teacher       & \code{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct}  & 4-bit via MLX; generates candidate boards \\
Embeddings    & \code{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}     & 384-d, L2-normalised; torch or OpenVINO IR \\
Student       & $769 \to 192 \to 95$ MLP    & LayerNorm, ReLU, sigmoid + BCE \\
Selection     & MMR                         & symbolic, identical in both paths \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The selection policy is deliberately \emph{not} learned. Distillation replaces only the
expensive candidate-generation step; the same symbolic MMR, answer quota and pinned core
run afterwards in both the teacher and student paths. This is what makes the two
comparable at all.

%==============================================================================
\section{Distillation}
%==============================================================================

The teacher produces good boards and takes roughly seven seconds, which is unusable in
conversation. Its boards therefore became a training set: 600 rows, 559 after
deduplication, split into train / validation / held-out with 84 rows never seen during
training.

The student consumes a 769-dimensional context vector --- sentence embeddings of the
scene and transcript plus a small number of flags --- and emits a score per vocabulary
word. Its entire job is to propose candidates quickly enough that the symbolic policy
can run in real time.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Student board latency, end to end   & 10.135\,ms \\
Teacher board latency               & 7.05\,s ($n=8$) \\
Measured speedup                    & $695\times$ \\
Functional diversity retained       & 95.3\,\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Caveat on the speedup.} It rests on eight teacher samples, and teacher latency
drifts run to run: 7.05\,s as committed, with 10.64\,s and 11.68\,s observed on loaded
machines. Where a claim must be safe, quote $>\!400\times$; quote $695\times$ only with
the sample count attached.

\section{Evaluation}
%==============================================================================

Boards are scored on a held-out split the student never trained on. The metrics are
anti-gaming by construction: a system that returns the same excellent board every time
should score badly, and this one is measured on whether it does.

\begin{center}
\small\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Gate} & \textbf{Student} & \textbf{Teacher} & \textbf{Verdict} \\
\midrule
Distinct functions @7            & 4.095  & 4.298  & \PASS\ (0.953 retained) \\
Near-duplicate rate              & 0.000  & 0.0006 & \PASS\ ($\le 0.08$) \\
Board variety                    & 0.952  & ---    & \PASS\ (80/84 distinct) \\
Coverage of intent (dynamic)     & 0.789  & 0.873  & \INC\ ($0.904\times$, margin $+0.003$) \\
Same-function-pair rate          & 0.1893 & 0.1735 & \FAIL\ (bar: $\le$ teacher) \\
Teacher word agreement           & 0.067  & ---    & report-only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two of these are not passes, and the project reports them as such.

\paragraph{The same-function-pair gate has failed through three review rounds.} The
student places slightly more same-function words on a board than the teacher does. The
open-answer path reaches the teacher's rate only at an aggression setting where coverage
of intent falls below its own bar. The two are in tension and neither has been resolved.

\paragraph{Coverage of intent clears its bar by 0.003.} The harness scores that margin
inconclusive at $n=71$. The papers say inconclusive, not pass.

\subsection{Where the diversity comes from}

Against a likelihood-ranked baseline over the same candidate pool ($n=120$), the diverse
selection policy roughly doubles the distinct communicative functions on a board and
nearly eliminates near-duplicates.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Top-$k$ baseline} & \textbf{Diverse policy} \\
\midrule
Distinct functions @7        & 3.875   & 4.683 \\
Near-duplicate rate          & 0.02738 & 0.000397 \\
Coverage of intent           & 0.775   & 1.000 \\
Exact match to oracle word   & 0.333   & 0.525 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent That near-duplicate figure is a $69\times$ reduction: the diverse policy almost
never puts two ways of saying the same thing on one board.

\section{The closed-vocabulary ceiling}
\label{sec:ceiling}
%==============================================================================

The first student agreed with the teacher's exact word choices 23\,\% of the time
against a target of 45\,\%. The obvious hypothesis was data volume. Enlarging the corpus
made agreement \emph{worse} --- down to 16\,\%, and later 6.7\,\% --- while every
diversity and speed gate continued to pass.

The cause is arithmetic, not optimisation. The teacher speaks an open vocabulary; the
student selects from a closed list of 95 words. Counting how much of the teacher's
output that list can represent at all:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Teacher word slots representable in the vocabulary & 32.5\,\% \\
Of those, hedge or filler                          & 49.1\,\% \\
Rows with zero representable dynamic words         & 12.2\,\% \\
Ceiling at $8.5\times$ the vocabulary (800 words)  & 56.6\,\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Word overlap was measuring the size of the label space, not the quality of the student.
Adding data moved the student toward the most frequent representable words, which are
disproportionately filler, so agreement fell while the boards themselves did not get
worse. Coverage of intent became the gated metric and word agreement was demoted to
report-only.

\paragraph{The remedy is not a longer list.} Concrete answers are now extracted directly
from the scene or transcript by an open-vocabulary extractor, so a word that was never in
the vocabulary can still reach the board --- which is how \emph{aunt} appears in the
seating example. On the held-out split this path fires on only 2 of 84 rows, so it moves
no aggregate gate. On a purpose-built set of 60 where / who / which / colour /
alternative questions whose answer is present in the context:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Deictic question set ($n=60$)} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Open-answer path fires                 & 88.3\,\% \\
Correct answer placed on the board     & 81.7\,\% \\
Closed vocabulary alone                & 1.7\,\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent``How many'' questions fire on 0 of 6 by design; counting is not attempted.

\section{Intel and OpenVINO}
%==============================================================================

The seam exists so the inference backend can move, and it has. The student exports to
ONNX, converts to OpenVINO IR at $1.43\times10^{-6}$ logit parity, and quantizes to INT8
with NNCF calibrated on 360 real feature vectors rather than random noise. A vocabulary
hash ships beside every artifact, so an IR built against a different word list fails
loudly instead of silently serving the wrong words.

\subsection{The result that holds up}

The same unmodified IR was run on an Intel Core Ultra 9 185H. Compared against PyTorch
\emph{on the same machine}, the OpenVINO runtime inverts by CPU vendor:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Student head, 500 iterations} & \textbf{PyTorch} & \textbf{OpenVINO FP32} & \textbf{Result} \\
\midrule
Apple M4 (ARM CPU plugin) & 0.015\,ms  & 0.025\,ms  & $1.7\times$ \emph{slower} \\
Intel Core Ultra 9 185H   & 0.1075\,ms & 0.0404\,ms & $2.7\times$ \textbf{faster} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This is a within-machine ratio, so it is immune to the OS, Python and OpenVINO version
differences between the two hosts. It says the toolchain is doing genuinely
Intel-specific work, and that the ARM figures elsewhere should be read as a portability
check rather than as deployment performance.

\subsection{Where the time actually goes}

The student head is roughly 0.1\,ms of a 10\,ms board. The real cost is MiniLM, a
22M-parameter transformer on the critical path. Moving \emph{it} to OpenVINO is the
largest single win in the project, and it reproduces on every machine measured:
$1.76\times$ on the Mac, $2.02\times$ on Intel CPU, $1.85\times$ on the Arc iGPU.

\subsection{What does not work}

The NPU (Intel AI Boost) is enumerated but both OpenVINO backends fail to compile
against it:

\begin{quote}
\footnotesize\code{Compilation failed.}\\
\code{ZE\_RESULT\_ERROR\_INVALID\_NULL\_POINTER}\\
\code{[NPU\_VCL] Compiler returned msg:}\\
\code{Missing upper bound for one or more nodes.}
\end{quote}

The NPU compiler requires statically shaped graphs; the IR carries a dynamic batch axis
so that one artifact serves both single-board inference and batched evaluation. A
batch-1 static export would very likely compile. It has not been done, and it is not
claimed.

\subsection{Honest accounting}

INT8 is a footprint result, not a speed result: 236\,KB against 662\,KB, a $2.8\times$
reduction, while being marginally \emph{slower} than FP32 on both machines.

Absolute Intel board latencies are withdrawn. That measurement session shared the machine
with a large model download and a concurrent teacher run; the identical PyTorch
configuration varied from 40 to 129\,ms across three runs against 18\,ms on an earlier
quiet run. Only the within-machine ratios above are quoted.

The speedup ratio against the teacher remains Mac-only. The teacher was run once on the
Core Ultra (median 170.93\,s per board, CPU-only \code{transformers}) purely to
demonstrate that it runs there; pairing that with the student would produce a
$\sim$6000$\times$ figure comparing an optimised student against an unoptimised teacher,
which is not a credible claim.

\section{Privacy}
%==============================================================================

A body-worn camera and microphone in a room containing other people is the central
ethical problem of this project. The response is structural rather than a policy
document.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Local only.} Every model runs on the Mac. No audio, video or transcript
  is transmitted anywhere.
  \item \textbf{Nothing persists.} Buffers are ephemeral. The privacy endpoint
  reports no media artifacts on disk, and audio and video as not persisted.
  \item \textbf{A hardware kill switch.} A debounced physical button (GPIO 14, internal
  pull-up, active-low, 50\,ms debounce) toggles capture on the device. While paused the
  I2S DMA still drains, so timing stays clean across pause and resume, but nothing is
  converted or transmitted and no frames are grabbed.
  \item \textbf{A visible indicator.} An LED is lit whenever capture is active, as a
  signal to people nearby.
  \item \textbf{A software pause} that drops incoming data before it reaches any buffer.
  \item \textbf{Pseudonymisation tooling} for the tap log, and a proxy consent template
  for a guardian of a participant who cannot consent for themselves.
\end{itemize}

Until written proxy consent and pseudonym handling are in place, the system is built and
tested on the project team only.

\paragraph{Open items.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The same-function-pair gate fails, and the available fix trades against coverage
  of intent.
  \item Coverage of intent clears its bar by a margin the harness calls inconclusive.
  \item The NPU rejects the dynamic-shape IR; a static batch-1 export is untried.
  \item Absolute Intel board latencies were taken on a loaded machine and are withdrawn.
  \item The vocabulary-sweep analysis behind the ceiling result has no committed script,
  and is computed at 94 words while the shipped vocabulary is 95.
  \item The wearable has never been tested with a real tablet on a real network.
  Everything above the device is proven.
\end{itemize}

\code{bash mac/demo.sh}\hfill real wearable, real models\\
\code{bash mac/demo.sh -{}-fake}\hfill no ESP32 required\\
