\begin{abstract}
VoiceLink is a wearable communication aid for nonverbal and minimally-verbal
people who use augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) boards. A
\$15-class body-worn device (ESP32-S3 camera + I$^2$S microphone) streams what
is happening around the user to a nearby computer, which transcribes the
conversation, describes the scene, combines both with a private personal-context
store, and pushes the vocabulary that fits \emph{this moment} onto a
tablet-hosted AAC board in real time. No caregiver pre-programs the board.
All inference is local. The system is built around a single architectural seam,
\code{rank\_vocabulary(scene, transcript, context)}, through which every model
call passes, so the inference backend has already been swapped from a
PyTorch/MLX development path to an OpenVINO INT8 path without touching the
firmware, server or UI. This document describes the hardware, the software
pipeline, the evaluation methodology and its measured results, one negative
result that we consider the most interesting scientific finding so far, and the
questions on which we would value a mentor's guidance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and motivation}
People who cannot speak often communicate through an AAC board: a grid of words
and pictograms they tap. The words on the board must be chosen in advance by a
caregiver or speech-language pathologist, who is guessing what the user will need.
The guess is frequently wrong, changing it takes another person and time, and
the user is left without the one word the conversation actually called for.
The concrete case we use throughout the project: a teacher asks
``\emph{what do plants take in from the air?}'' --- no pre-programmed board has
\emph{carbon dioxide} on it.

VoiceLink removes the guessing. The aid senses the situation and assembles the
board live. The design goal is not to predict \emph{the} word the user will say
(the AAC literature on keystroke savings already optimises that) but to offer a
\emph{slate} of 10 words that spans the different communicative moves the user
might want to make --- answer, reject, request, ask back, comment --- so that
whichever one they mean is reachable in one tap.

Target population is autism-first, extending to aphasia, ALS and Parkinson's.
Development and testing are restricted to the team until written proxy consent
and pseudonym handling exist (Section~\ref{sec:privacy}).

\section{System architecture}
\subsection{Three devices, one seam}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.27\linewidth}p{0.68\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Device} & \textbf{Role} \\ \midrule
Wearable capture device (ESP32-S3) & Senses and streams: one VGA JPEG every 2\,s and continuous 16\,kHz mono PCM over a single WebSocket. Does \emph{no} processing. Runs its own Wi-Fi access point so it needs no router. \\
Computer (Mac today; Intel mini-PC is the deployment target) & All intelligence: speech recognition, scene description, personal-context retrieval, vocabulary ranking and board selection, tap logging. Serves the AAC web app. \\
Tablet / phone & Shows the board as a plain-HTTP web page with a WebSocket for live updates. The user taps; every tap is logged with the context that produced the board. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The wearable talks only to the computer; the tablet talks only to the computer.
Nothing leaves the local network.

\subsection{The seam}
Every model call in the system goes through one function with a fixed signature
(\file{mac/rank\_vocabulary.py}):
\begin{lstlisting}
rank_vocabulary(scene, transcript, context, strategy=...) -> list[RankedWord]
\end{lstlisting}
The firmware never calls a model; the web app never calls a model; the server
reaches intelligence only through this function. The \code{strategy} argument
selects the policy (\code{topk}, \code{diverse}, \code{student}) and environment
flags select the runtime underneath it
(\code{VOICELINK\_STUDENT\_BACKEND=torch|openvino},
\code{VOICELINK\_EMBED\_BACKEND}, \code{VOICELINK\_OV\_DEVICE=CPU|GPU|NPU}).
This is what allowed the Intel/OpenVINO port (Section~\ref{sec:intel}) to be a
configuration change rather than a rewrite, and it is the property we would
most like to preserve as the models change.

\section{Hardware}
\subsection{Wearable capture device}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Board:} Freenove ESP32-S3-WROOM CAM (dual-core Xtensa LX7 at 240\,MHz, 8\,MB OPI PSRAM, OV2640-class camera on the \code{ESP32S3\_EYE} pin map).
\item \textbf{Microphone:} Knowles SPH0645 I$^2$S MEMS microphone (BCLK GPIO\,1, WS GPIO\,2, DIN GPIO\,42; SEL tied to GND for the left channel). The SPH0645 delivers 18-bit samples left-justified in 32-bit words; the firmware reads raw words and arithmetic-shifts by 14 bits to 16-bit PCM.
\item \textbf{Capture cadence:} VGA (640$\times$480) JPEG at quality~12 every 2000\,ms; audio in 512-sample chunks (32\,ms) at 16\,kHz.
\item \textbf{Indicators and controls:} the on-board WS2812 LED is lit red whenever capture is active, as the visible signal to bystanders; a debounced push-button on GPIO\,14 toggles capture on and off (the hardware kill switch).
\item \textbf{Network:} SoftAP \code{VoiceLink-Capture} on channel~6, WebSocket server on \code{ws://192.168.4.1:81/}. Binary frames carry a 1-byte type tag: \code{0x01} + JPEG, \code{0x02} + PCM16LE.
\item \textbf{Concurrency:} the I$^2$S read-and-send loop is a high-priority FreeRTOS task pinned to core~0; camera capture, WebSocket servicing and the button run in \code{loop()} on core~1, so a slow JPEG grab can never stutter the audio.
\item \textbf{Build:} Arduino-ESP32 core, FQBN \code{esp32:esp32:esp32s3} with \code{PSRAM=opi}, \code{PartitionScheme=huge\_app}, \code{USBMode=hwcdc}; the current firmware compiles to 1.0\,MB (31\,\% of flash). Source: \file{firmware/voicelink\_capture/voicelink\_capture.ino} (361 lines). A separate bench rig (\file{firmware/mic\_test/}) records raw I$^2$S audio over serial for verifying the microphone wiring before trusting the stream.
\item \textbf{Cost:} the board and microphone are in the \$15--20 range at single-unit prices; a formal bill of materials has not yet been written (flagged as unsourced in our numbers inventory).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Host computer}
Development host is an Apple M4 Mac. The deployment target is an Intel
mini-PC / AI PC (Core Ultra with NPU, or a small Xeon) running the same Python
server with the OpenVINO backend selected. No Intel-hardware measurements exist
yet; Section~\ref{sec:intel} describes exactly what has and has not been measured.

\section{Software}
All host code is Python (\file{mac/}, about 7\,000 lines including evaluation),
served with \code{aiohttp}. The audio path is non-blocking by construction:
model calls run in worker threads and the board is pushed when ready.

\subsection{Perception pipeline (\file{mac/perception/})}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Wearable client} connects to the device's WebSocket and demultiplexes the two streams into ephemeral in-memory buffers. Nothing is written to disk.
\item \textbf{Audio worker} runs an energy VAD (30\,ms frames, RMS threshold 350, 600\,ms trailing silence, 300\,ms minimum utterance) to find utterance boundaries, then transcribes each complete utterance. A near-field loudness heuristic (RMS $>$ 800) labels speech as \emph{partner} (addressed to the user) versus \emph{ambient}; questions flip an \code{addressed} flag that downstream selection uses.
\item \textbf{Transcriber} is behind an abstract interface: \code{faster-whisper} \code{base.en} INT8 on CPU by default; an OpenVINO GenAI \code{WhisperPipeline} backend (whisper-base.en, INT8 weights) is the Intel path. Measured faster-whisper latency on the Mac: 0.33\,s for a 2.5\,s clip, 0.46\,s for 5.2\,s (real-time factor 0.09--0.13 on short utterances).
\item \textbf{Vision worker} polls the latest frame every 0.5\,s, runs a 64$\times$64 grayscale change detector (0.21\,ms per frame) and only invokes the VLM when the mean absolute difference exceeds 12 and at least 2\,s have elapsed. The VLM is Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (4-bit, MLX on the Mac) prompted for one sentence about what the user might want to talk about; frames are downscaled to $\le$768\,px (4.1\,s per description at 768\,px, 2.1\,s at 512\,px; the gate keeps the duty cycle low).
\item \textbf{Context state} holds the rolling transcript, last utterance, addressed flag and current scene sentence, and exposes a \code{snapshot()} that is what the seam receives.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Personal context}
A local JSON profile (\file{mac/personal\_context.json}, never committed, never transmitted) carries identity, relationships, routines, today's events with vocabulary hints, and sensory notes. \file{personal\_context\_loader.py} slices the profile against the current scene and transcript (name matches, event windows, noise-sensitivity triggers) so the prompt sees only the relevant part.

\subsection{Ranking and board composition}
\label{sec:ranking}
The \code{diverse} strategy is the production teacher policy:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Over-generate} a pool with a local LLM (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, 4-bit) using a slot-based prompt: exactly one first-person phrase for each of nine AAC communicative functions (request, reject, comment, question, share-information, express-feeling, social, direct-attention, clarify) plus four phrases on the concrete topics in play. Slot prompting is what stops the model mode-collapsing onto the single likeliest reply.
\item \textbf{Inject} a deterministic core pool (\emph{yes}, \emph{no}, \emph{I don't know}, \emph{wait}, \emph{I need help}) so a bad generation can never strand the user.
\item \textbf{Select} with maximal marginal relevance (MMR) over MiniLM sentence embeddings (\code{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, 384-d), with per-candidate function tags so the selection spans functions, a low $\lambda$ (0.35) for answers to a question so that answers spread across distinct responses rather than paraphrases, an answer-quota reservation when a question is active, and a hedge cap so ``I'm not sure'' variants cannot fill the board.
\item \textbf{Assemble} four pinned core words (yes / no / I need help / wait --- four distinct functions, so ``never mode-collapsed'' is structurally true) plus six dynamic words, padded to exactly ten.
\end{enumerate}
Since RC-3 (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}) an \emph{open-vocabulary answer extractor}
(\file{evaluation/phase4/answers.py}) reads concrete answers out of the context
for alternative (``A or B?'') and deictic (where/who/which) questions and
reserves slots for them, so that an answer like \emph{window seat} can reach the
board even though it is in no fixed vocabulary.

\subsection{AAC web app}
A single-origin page (\file{mac/web/index.html}) served over plain HTTP on the
LAN (HTTPS would hit the mixed-content wall with the device's \code{ws://}
stream), with ARASAAC pictograms cached locally. Every tap is sent back and
appended to \file{mac/taps.jsonl} together with the board id and the full
context snapshot that produced the board. This interaction log is the dataset
that will eventually replace synthetic evaluation and it has been collected
from the first UI version. A second page (\file{console.html}) is a developer
console: scenario injection, profile switching, a phase dashboard, and the
ability to push a real image through the real VLM.

\section{Evaluation methodology}
\label{sec:eval}
We found early that the obvious metric --- does the board contain the oracle
word --- is easy to game (a board of ten synonyms of the likeliest answer scores
well and is useless). The harness therefore uses slate metrics chosen to resist
that failure:
\begin{description}
\item[Distinct communicative functions @7] how many of the nine AAC functions the top seven words span, classified by multi-exemplar nearest-centroid in embedding space (a single-centroid version mislabelled \emph{yes} and \emph{carbon dioxide} as ``I don't know''; fixed and applied to both arms).
\item[Near-duplicate pair rate] fraction of word pairs with cosine $\ge$ a threshold.
\item[Same-function-pair rate] fraction of pairs sharing a function --- the direct measure of ``seven paraphrases''.
\item[Coverage-of-intent] fraction of scenarios where at least one of the authored \emph{acceptable moves} is expressible from the board. We report it on the \emph{dynamic} slice only, because the four pinned core words self-certify on the full board.
\item[Board variety] distinct boards over the held-out set (a degeneracy guard).
\item[Teacher agreement] word-level overlap of student and teacher dynamic words --- reported, not gated, for the reason in Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}.
\end{description}
Judging uses an LLM and is labelled \emph{proxy}; a human-comparison hook exists
but no human study has been run. Every evaluation is on \textbf{synthetic
scenarios}: 120 phase-3 scenarios across five settings, and a 600-row teacher
corpus (authored scenarios plus earlier phases) that is embedding-deduplicated
at cosine 0.95, category-capped at 15\,\%, split 391/84/84 with a fingerprint
so a drifted corpus refuses to score against a stale partition. We say this
plainly in every document: a student that passes here may be imitating the
teacher imitating an LLM.

Two process findings from this phase that we think are generally useful:
\begin{itemize}
\item The raw student \emph{argmax} board was measured useless (hedge walls: 2.1 hedge tokens per top-7, 72.6\,\% of boards with $\ge$2 hedges). The fix was architectural, not more training: treat the student's logits as a \emph{candidate pool} and run the same symbolic MMR selection the teacher uses over it. Hedges fell to 0.012 per board; the device now runs exactly the policy the harness measures.
\item 5$\times$ more distillation data made teacher agreement \emph{worse} (0.232 $\to$ 0.165), falsifying the data-volume hypothesis. The reason is Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Latency engineering (23 August 2026)}
An end-to-end benchmark through the seam (\file{mac/intel/bench\_e2e.py}, 84
held-out rows, three runtime combinations, hardware fingerprint recorded)
showed that 71 of 73\,ms per board was MiniLM re-embedding the static 94-word
vocabulary on every call. Memoising embeddings per (backend, text) and batching
scene+transcript into one encoder call brought a board to \textbf{15.3\,ms p50}
on the fully-OpenVINO path (embed + head + selection; context texts evicted
between rounds so every board still encodes a never-seen scene and
transcript). The OpenVINO MiniLM export matches the PyTorch embeddings at cosine
1.000 and encodes a single text in 5.6\,ms versus 16.0\,ms. Re-running the full harness the same day gave a 12--15\,ms student against a 10.6--11.7\,s teacher; the teacher timing drifts run to run, so the table quotes a range.

\section{The closed-vocabulary ceiling (negative result)}
\label{sec:ceiling}
The distillation target is an open-vocabulary teacher (an LLM that can write
\emph{carbon dioxide}, \emph{window seat}, \emph{Ms.~Lee}); the student is a
classifier over a closed label set built by frequency from the teacher's own
boards (minimum frequency 3, maximum 200, embedding-deduplicated at $\tau=0.80$,
giving 94 words). Measured over the 3\,600 dynamic word slots the teacher
produced:
\begin{itemize}
\item the teacher used 2\,362 distinct dynamic words, 94.8\,\% of which appear fewer than three times;
\item the 94-word vocabulary can express only \textbf{32.5\,\%} of the teacher's word choices, and \textbf{49.1\,\%} of what it can express is hedge/filler (\emph{I'm not sure}, \emph{maybe}, \ldots);
\item 12.2\,\% of rows have \emph{no} representable dynamic word at all;
\item growing the vocabulary helps only slowly: 292 words $\to$ 42.5\,\%, 752 words (8$\times$) $\to$ 55.3\,\%, 800 words $\to$ 56.6\,\%; full coverage needs all 2\,362.
\end{itemize}
This explains the data-volume result: every added row contributes mostly
one-off concrete words, so a frequency-built vocabulary drifts \emph{further}
toward filler as data grows, and the achievable agreement ceiling (0.325) is
low by construction --- the student's 0.165 was already half of it. The lesson
generalises to any closed-label distillation of an open-vocabulary teacher whose
useful outputs are long-tail. The fix we shipped is architectural: read concrete
answers out of the context (Section~\ref{sec:ranking}) instead of from a label
space. Its honest status: it fires on only 2 of 84 held-out rows, so its effect
on the gates is noise; the claim is ``the mechanism works and nothing regressed'',
and a deictic-question evaluation set is being built to measure it properly.

\section{Deployment path on Intel (OpenVINO)}
\label{sec:intel}
\begin{itemize}
\item Student: PyTorch $\to$ ONNX (opset 17, standard layers only) $\to$ OpenVINO IR (max logit delta vs ONNX $1.4\times10^{-6}$) $\to$ NNCF post-training INT8 on 360 real calibration contexts: 661\,KB $\to$ 235\,KB (2.8$\times$), top-6 overlap with FP32 0.950 mean / 0.667 min, max logit delta 1.05.
\item MiniLM: exported with \code{optimum-cli} (tokenizer, encoder and pooling in one graph); serves the MMR embeddings torch-free.
\item Whisper: whisper-base.en INT8 through \code{openvino\_genai.WhisperPipeline}; on the Mac's ARM plugin it is \emph{slower} than faster-whisper (1.16\,s vs 0.68\,s p50 on a 2\,s clip, $n=10$, identical transcripts); an earlier one-clip 0.35 vs 0.42\,s figure did not reproduce and was withdrawn. Its value is the Intel CPU/GPU/NPU port, which is unmeasured.
\item Serving: \code{VOICELINK\_STUDENT\_BACKEND=openvino} compiles the INT8 IR (FP32 fallback) behind the seam, with a vocabulary-hash sidecar so a stale artifact refuses to serve wrong words; \code{VOICELINK\_OV\_DEVICE} targets CPU, GPU or the Core Ultra NPU with the same file.
\item Pitfall worth knowing: OpenVINO's ARM CPU plugin defaults to f16 inference, which made the Whisper decoder degenerate and drifted logits by $10^{-2}$; we pin \code{INFERENCE\_PRECISION\_HINT=f32} on CPU.
\item \textbf{What does not exist:} any measurement on Intel silicon. Every number above is Apple M-series through the ARM plugin. The benchmark scripts self-label the hardware so a run on an Intel box is one command.
\end{itemize}

\section{Privacy, consent and responsible use}
\label{sec:privacy}
The wearable captures audio and images that include bystanders who have not
consented. Our response is enforced in the architecture rather than in policy
text: inference is local-only; audio and frame buffers are ephemeral (no
persistent recording; nothing is written to disk); the device has a physical
capture toggle and a visible recording LED; the personal-context store never
leaves the host; the VLM produces a one-sentence scene description and no face
detection or identification is performed; the only persisted data is the tap
log, which the user's own taps create. Testing is team-only until written proxy
consent and pseudonym handling exist. The model \emph{suggests}; the user's tap
is always the utterance, and four safety words are pinned outside model
control. Synthetic-scenario evaluation and LLM judging are documented bias
risks. Generative-AI tooling used in development (Claude Code, Codex) is
disclosed in the project documentation.

\section{Status, open problems, and where we would value guidance}
\textbf{Status.} Phases 0--5 (guardrails, live pipeline, console, self-test
harness, distillation, Intel readiness) are complete; 172 automated tests pass.
Phase 6 (integration demo and video) is in progress. A patent novelty brief
exists; nothing has been filed.

\textbf{Open problems.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Evaluation validity.} Everything is synthetic and LLM-judged. What is the smallest human study (team-internal first, then consented pilot) that would give a defensible number, and which of our slate metrics would you expect to survive contact with real AAC users?
\item \emph{The ceiling.} Is closed-label distillation the wrong frame entirely? Alternatives we are weighing: a retrieval student over an open candidate store; a small sequence model that emits words rather than classes; distilling the \emph{selection policy} (MMR weights, quotas) rather than the word choices.
\item \emph{Turn-taking latency budget.} With boards at 15\,ms the bottleneck is now perception (0.3--0.5\,s speech, 2--4\,s scene). How should the board update policy trade freshness against stability for a user who is scanning it?
\item \emph{Bystander privacy.} What would a defensible consent model look like for a continuously sensing assistive wearable in a classroom?
\item \emph{Venues.} We are preparing short workshop submissions (on-device intelligence; efficient deep learning) and an Intel AI Global Impact Festival entry. Which framing --- systems, negative result, or accessibility --- do you think is strongest?
\end{enumerate}
