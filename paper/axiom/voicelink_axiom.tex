\title{The Closed-Vocabulary Ceiling in Distilling\\an Open-Vocabulary Teacher}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)\\Affiliation\\\texttt{email}}

\begin{abstract}
A common efficiency recipe distils an open-vocabulary generator (an LLM) into
a small classifier over a closed label set built from the teacher's most
frequent outputs. We report a negative result from doing this for a real-time
assistive-communication task and explain it with a counting argument.
Five-fold more distillation data made student--teacher agreement \emph{worse}
(0.232 $\to$ 0.165). Measurement shows why: the teacher's useful outputs are
heavy-tailed (2\,330 distinct words over 3\,600 output slots, 95.5\,\% seen
fewer than three times), so a frequency-built 94-label vocabulary represents
only 32.4\,\% of the teacher's choices, half of that is hedge/filler, and
11.7\,\% of examples have no representable target. Coverage grows only
logarithmically in vocabulary size ($\approx$0.12 per $e$-fold; an $8\times$
larger label space reaches 55\,\%). Agreement was therefore capped at 0.324
before training, and the student had already reached 51\,\% of that cap. A toy
model shows that under such a tail, added data tilts a frequency-built
vocabulary toward filler. We propose the \emph{representability ceiling}---the
fraction of teacher output slots expressible in the student's label space---as
a one-line pre-training diagnostic, and describe an architectural remedy (read
long-tail answers from the context, not a label space) whose status we report
candidly: it fires on 2 of 84 held-out rows, so its gate effects are noise; no
gate regressed.
\end{abstract}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
Knowledge distillation~\citep{hinton2015} is the workhorse of efficient
deployment. When the teacher is an open-vocabulary generator and the student
must run in milliseconds on-device, a natural and very cheap student is a
classifier over a \emph{closed} label set: collect the teacher's outputs, keep
the labels occurring at least $f$ times, cap the set at $V$, train a softmax
head. It is far cheaper than a generative student, and ``the vocabulary was
built from the teacher, so it must fit the teacher.'' This paper is about the
case where that reasoning fails, and how cheaply the failure can be predicted.
Our setting is augmentative and alternative communication (AAC): a wearable
senses the conversation around a non-speaking user, and a model must place the
right words on a communication board within a conversational turn. The teacher is an LLM pipeline that
composes a ten-word board (four pinned core words plus six \emph{dynamic}
words) from an ASR transcript and a scene description; the student is a
two-layer MLP over sentence embeddings with a 94-way output head, ${>}400\times$
faster than the teacher. The student is fast and diverse. It does not agree
with the teacher, and giving it more teacher data made agreement \emph{fall}.
Our contributions are deliberately simple: (1)~the documented negative result
(\S\ref{sec:obs}); (2)~its measured root cause---a heavy-tailed teacher whose
outputs a frequency-built vocabulary covers only logarithmically in its size
(\S\ref{sec:cause}); (3)~a toy model, labelled as such, in which added data
pushes such a vocabulary \emph{toward} filler (\S\ref{sec:model}); (4)~an
architectural remedy reported candidly (\S\ref{sec:remedy}) and a one-line
pre-training diagnostic, the \emph{representability ceiling}
(\S\ref{sec:general}).

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Teacher.} Given a scene description (from a small VLM) and a
transcript (from local ASR), the teacher LLM%
\footnote{\texttt{Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct} (4-bit), run locally; the scene
describer is the VL variant of the same family~\citep{qwen25vl}.}
proposes candidate words, and a symbolic maximal-marginal-relevance
(MMR)~\citep{mmr1998} stage selects six dynamic words for diversity of
communicative function. The teacher corpus is 600 rows of synthetic scenarios
(authored plus earlier phases), each with exactly six dynamic words, i.e.\
$3\,600$ teacher output slots; after embedding-deduplication and a per-category
cap, 559 rows remain, split 391/84/84 (train/val/test) with a fingerprint that
refuses to score a drifted corpus against a stale partition.

\paragraph{Student.} Input is a 769-d vector (384-d MiniLM~\citep{minilm}
scene and transcript embeddings plus an \texttt{is\_question} bit); the network
is LayerNorm $\to$ Linear($769{\to}192$) $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Dropout(0.3) $\to$
Linear($192{\to}94$). The 94 labels are the \emph{dynamic vocabulary}: teacher
board words with frequency $\ge3$, capped at 200, embedding-deduplicated at
cosine 0.80 (22 canonical merges). At inference the logits are a candidate
pool over which the teacher's MMR selection is run; all metrics judge the top
seven of the ten-word board.

\paragraph{Metrics.} We track slate-quality gates (distinct communicative
functions, near-duplicate rate, same-function-pair rate, board variety,
LLM-judged coverage-of-intent on the dynamic slice) and a \emph{teacher
agreement} metric, \texttt{dynamic\_overlap}: the fraction of the student's
dynamic words that are also among the teacher's dynamic words for the same
row. Agreement is the metric this paper is about; the gates are in
Appendix~\ref{app:gates}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Observation: more data, less agreement}
\label{sec:obs}
The first student (v2), trained on the authored portion of the corpus,
reached \texttt{dynamic\_overlap} $=0.232$ against a gate of $\ge 0.45$. The
obvious hypothesis was data volume. The corpus was enlarged with rows from
earlier phases---logged at the time as a $5\times$ increase in distillation
data%
\footnote{That multiplier is not reconcilable against the committed corpora,
which hold 360 authored scenarios and 600 teacher rows (559 after dedup), a
$1.7\times$ increase in rows. We report the discrepancy rather than resolve it;
the argument below does not depend on the exact factor.}%
---and the student retrained (v2.1, held-out $n=87$). Agreement fell to
$0.165$; the diversity and speed gates all passed. A later variant with a
richer input and MMR over logits fell further, to $0.056$, at which point
agreement was demoted to report-only. The $0.232\to0.165$ pair is a
documented historical run (no result file is committed for v2/v2.1); 0.056 is
reproducible from the committed model. Capacity, optimisation and noise were entertained as explanations; none
predicts a \emph{decrease}.

\section{Root cause: the teacher's outputs are not in the label space}
\label{sec:cause}
This section is pure counting over the teacher corpus and the vocabulary
file; no model is run. A slot is \emph{representable} if its word (lower-cased,
or its canonical-merge image) is in the label set; \emph{filler} is a hedge
(\emph{maybe}, \emph{I'm not sure}). The corpus was independently regenerated
once (same contexts, fresh teacher decoding); every number in this section moved
by less than $0.006$, so the ceiling is a property of the task, not of one
sampling run.

Table~\ref{tab:repr} gives the result. The teacher used 2\,330 distinct
dynamic words across 3\,600 slots (mean frequency 1.5), and 95.5\,\% of its
types fall below the floor that admits a label. Only 32.4\,\% of slots are
expressible by the student at all, and 48.6\,\% of those are hedges; one row in
eight has \emph{no} representable target, so its training signal is noise with
respect to the teacher's intent. A student word can only match a representable
slot, so \texttt{dynamic\_overlap} is bounded above by $0.324$: v2.1's 0.165
was already 51\,\% of what any 94-way classifier could achieve, although
against the $\ge0.45$ gate it read as a 16\,\% failure.

\paragraph{Does a bigger label space fix it?} Holding the corpus fixed, we
rebuilt the vocabulary by frequency at several sizes (no deduplication) and
measured coverage $\covv(V)$ of the 3\,600 slots (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}).


Coverage is closely logarithmic in $V$ over the usable range,
$\covv(V)\approx0.123\ln V-0.259$: an output layer $8\times$ the shipped one
still represents only 55.5\,\% of the teacher's choices, and full coverage
needs all 2\,330 types, i.e.\ the teacher's output set memorised. This is what
a Zipf-like~\citep{zipf1949} distribution predicts: with rank-$r$ probability
$\propto r^{-s}$, $s\approx1$, the mass in the top $V$ types is
$H_V/H_N\approx\ln V/\ln N$, slope $1/\ln2330\approx0.13$ per $e$-fold against
the fitted $0.123$. (A single-exponent Zipf fit to all six points is worse,
max error 0.06 at $s\approx0.6$, because the filler head is heavier than a
pure power law; the log-linear description is the operationally useful one.)

Coverage is thus governed by the shape of the teacher's output distribution,
not by any parameter the student designer controls cheaply. A closed-vocabulary
classifier is the wrong instrument for the long-tail half of the task.

\section{Why more data makes it worse (toy model)}
\label{sec:model}
We give a mechanism in a deliberately simple model, labelled as a toy: it is
meant to make the direction of the effect intuitive, not to fit the numbers.

\paragraph{Model.} Each teacher slot is, with probability $\pi$, a
\emph{head} word from a small fixed set (hedges and general functions:
\emph{wait}, \emph{maybe}) and otherwise a \emph{concrete} word specific to the
scene (\emph{aunt}, \emph{right arm}). Concrete types seen after $n$ rows grow
as $D(n)\approx\alpha n^{\beta}$, $\beta$ near 1 (Heaps-type
growth~\citep{heaps1978}; here $2\,330$ types from $3\,600$ slots). The
vocabulary keeps types with count $\ge f$, capped at $V$.

\paragraph{Consequence 1: the ceiling does not rise with $n$.} Head words
always clear the floor; a concrete type clears it only if it recurs $f$ times.
With $\beta\to1$ the expected count of a typical concrete type stays $O(1)$ as
$n$ grows, so the representable fraction of concrete \emph{slots} converges to
a constant set by the tail shape, not by $n$: the ceiling tends to
$\covv_\infty\approx\pi+(1-\pi)\,\gamma$, with $\gamma$ the recurring share of
the tail. Measured here, the head is about half of the representable slots and
$\covv=0.324$, consistent with a small $\gamma$.

\paragraph{Consequence 2: the vocabulary tilts toward filler.} Which concrete
types clear the floor is close to a lottery among low-count types; as $n$
grows more of them clear it, but each one's conditional probability given the
context stays near $f/n\to0$. Training signal per concrete label does not grow
with data, whereas head labels accumulate signal linearly in $n$. A student
minimising cross-entropy over this label set puts its mass on the head, and
its top-$k$ is increasingly filler. Agreement, which needs the student's words
to coincide with the teacher's mostly-concrete dynamic words, therefore
\emph{falls} as the student becomes a better estimator of the only consistent
signal it is given. This matches \S\ref{sec:obs}, and the independent
observation that the same student's raw argmax board was a ``hedge wall''
(2.1 hedge tokens per top-7; 72.6\,\% of boards with $\ge2$ hedges) until
selection was changed to diversify over the logits.

The model says nothing about student capacity, and assumes $f,V$ fixed as
$n$ grows; re-tuning them changes constants, not the logarithmic shape of
\S\ref{sec:cause}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The architectural remedy, and its honest status}
\label{sec:remedy}
If concrete answers cannot live in a label space, they must come from
the \emph{context} at inference time: for an alternative question (``the window seat or the
aisle?'') the answers are the conjuncts of the question; for a deictic
wh-question (\emph{where}/\emph{who}/\emph{which}) they are multi-word spans
of the scene description, ranked against an answer-type prototype blended with
scene salience and explicitly \emph{not} against the question (an answer to
``where would you like to sit?'' resembles the answer space, not the
question). For every other question the open path stands down and the closed
student's board is unchanged: the closed student supplies fast, diverse general
functions; the open path supplies the long tail without enlarging the output
layer. (An earlier attempt that unioned single tokens scored against the
question vector cost 0.24 coverage; the question-type gate is what made the
mechanism safe.)

\paragraph{Status.} On the held-out split the open path fires on \textbf{2 of
84} rows (1 of 71 coverage-scored rows). Every before/after gate delta is
therefore one- or two-row noise and none is reported as an improvement. What
the numbers support is the negative claim: \emph{no gate regressed}, dynamic
coverage-of-intent clears its bar at $0.904\times$ (margin inconclusive), and the
previously unrepresentable probe (``who do you want to sit next to?'' $\to$
\emph{aunt}) is now answered. A deictic-question evaluation
set is the only way this mechanism becomes measurable. Extracted spans are by construction
not teacher vocabulary words, so word-overlap cannot reward this path.

\section{A pre-training diagnostic and when it applies}
\label{sec:general}
Nothing above used the student: the ceiling was computable from the teacher
corpus and the vocabulary file before a single gradient step, and would have
saved the data-volume experiment.

\paragraph{Representability ceiling.} For a teacher corpus with output slots
$S$ (one per teacher output token or label) and a student label space
$\mathcal{V}$ with canonicalisation map $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
\rho(\mathcal V)\;=\;\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\mathbf 1\!\left[\kappa(w_s)\in\mathcal V\right],
\qquad
\rho_{\text{useful}}(\mathcal V)\;=\;\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}\mathbf 1\!\left[\kappa(w_s)\in\mathcal V\;\wedge\;w_s\notin\text{filler}\right].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Any slot-level agreement metric is bounded by $\rho$, and any metric that
discounts filler by $\rho_{\text{useful}}$; here $\rho=0.324$ and
$\rho_{\text{useful}}=0.324\times(1-0.491)\approx0.165$ (that v2.1's agreement
coincides with it is, as far as we can tell, a coincidence). Reporting
agreement as a fraction of $\rho$ turns a misleading ``16\,\%'' into an
informative ``51\,\% of achievable''; the sweep $\rho(V)$, one line per size,
reveals the growth rate, and a logarithmic curve warns that no affordable
output layer will close the gap.

\paragraph{Scope.} The argument applies whenever (i) the teacher is
open-vocabulary, (ii) its \emph{useful} outputs are long-tail, and (iii) the
student's label space is built by frequency from the teacher's outputs; not
when useful outputs concentrate in a head (intent, sentiment), where
closed-label distillation is known to work. The failure is easy to miss
because vocabulary-building looks self-consistent---every label came from the
teacher---and gates that do not depend on agreement (diversity, speed) pass.
The diagnostic is cheap enough to be a default.

\section{Limitations}
All evaluation is on \textbf{synthetic scenarios}; the teacher and the
coverage-of-intent judge are both LLMs, so a student that passes here may be
imitating the teacher imitating an LLM. Single domain (AAC board composition),
single teacher, single student architecture, $n=600$ teacher rows (held-out
$n=84$). The $0.232\to0.165$ pair is a documented historical run, not a
reproducible artefact; the toy model is unvalidated beyond consistency with
the observations. No human subjects and no human interaction data; testing was
on the development team under a self-imposed consent policy. The counting
results of \S\ref{sec:cause} are, by contrast, exact and reproducible.

\paragraph{Use of LLMs.} (1)~The distillation teacher and the
coverage-of-intent judge are LLMs (\S\ref{sec:setup}); they are objects of
study, not sources of claims. (2)~The system and harness were developed
with AI-assisted coding tools; every number here was recomputed from committed
files. (3)~An LLM assisted with drafting
and editing; the authors take responsibility for all content.

\section{Reproducing the counting results}
\label{app:repro}
The numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:repr} and~\ref{tab:sweep} require no model
call. Dynamic slots are the board words not in the pinned core set; a slot is
representable if its lower-cased word, or its canonical-merge image, is in the
vocabulary; filler is decided by the harness's hedge list. The sweep rebuilds
the vocabulary with the corpus's own frequency-based builder at
$(f,V)\in\{(1,94),(3,200),(2,400),(1,752),(1,800),(1,10^4)\}$ and recomputes
coverage. The log fit is ordinary least squares of $\covv$ on $\ln V$ over the
five finite rows. The small discrepancy between 94-word coverage in
Table~\ref{tab:repr} (0.324, shipped vocabulary with canonical merges) and
Table~\ref{tab:sweep} (0.309, frequency-only rebuild) is the effect of the 22
canonical merges. The counting procedure is fully specified above and requires no model call.
